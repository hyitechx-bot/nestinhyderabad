Booking Amount for Flat
How Much to Pay & What to Know Before Paying

Before you hand over that cheque or make that transfer, here's everything you need to know about booking amounts — how much is normal, what documents to get, and your refund rights.

📝 Buyer Education•July 30, 2026•5 min read

What is a Booking Amount?

A booking amount (also called token amount or earnest money) is the initial payment you make to reserve a specific flat unit. It signals your serious intent to purchase and takes that unit off the market while you arrange financing and complete paperwork.

Think of it as a commitment from both sides — you commit to buying, and the developer commits to holding that unit for you.

How Much Should You Pay?

Booking amounts in Hyderabad typically range from 1% to 10% of the total flat cost, depending on the project stage and developer:

  • Token/Expression of Interest: ₹1-2 lakhs (just to hold the unit for a few days)
  • Standard Booking: 5-10% of the total agreement value
  • Premium/High-Demand Projects: Some developers ask for 10-15% upfront

Example: For a ₹90 lakh flat, expect a booking amount between ₹90,000 (1%) and ₹9 lakhs (10%).

Important: Under RERA, a developer cannot collect more than 10% of the flat cost as advance without executing a registered agreement of sale. If someone asks for more before the agreement, that's a red flag.

Is the Booking Amount Refundable?

This is the most common question — and the answer depends on the situation:

When You Can Get a Refund:

  • The developer fails to deliver on time (RERA allows full refund with interest)
  • The project doesn't have RERA registration (illegal collection — fully refundable)
  • Material changes to the project that weren't disclosed
  • Your loan gets rejected (if mentioned in the booking terms)

When Refund May Be Difficult:

  • You change your mind after booking (developer may deduct forfeiture charges)
  • The booking form has a "non-refundable" clause you signed
  • You fail to arrange payment within the agreed timeline

Typical forfeiture: Developers usually deduct 1-2% of the flat value or the entire token amount (whichever is lower) if you cancel voluntarily.

Documents You Must Get After Paying

Never pay a booking amount without receiving proper documentation. Insist on:

  • Official receipt on the company letterhead with the amount, date, and payment mode
  • Booking application form with flat number, floor, carpet area, and total price clearly mentioned
  • Cost breakdown sheet — base price, GST, maintenance deposit, parking charges, legal fees, etc.
  • Payment schedule — when future payments are due
  • RERA registration number of the project
  • Cancellation/refund policy in writing

Legal Aspects to Know

  • A booking form is a legally binding document — read every clause before signing
  • Under RERA, the Agreement for Sale must be executed within a reasonable time after booking
  • Always pay via cheque, bank transfer, or UPI — never cash (for your paper trail and tax records)
  • The booking amount is adjusted against your total flat price — it's not an extra charge
  • If paying before RERA registration, the developer is violating the law

Tips to Protect Yourself

  1. Never rush. A good flat won't vanish in 24 hours despite what a sales executive says
  2. Verify RERA status first. Do this before paying even ₹1
  3. Get the refund policy in writing — verbal assurances mean nothing legally
  4. Keep all communication records — WhatsApp messages, emails, brochures
  5. Don't pay to "block a price" unless you've done your due diligence
  6. Start your home loan pre-approval before booking to avoid rejection surprises

The booking amount is your first financial commitment in the home buying journey. Make sure you're informed and protected before you take this step.
