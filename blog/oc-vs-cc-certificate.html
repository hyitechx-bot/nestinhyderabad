OC vs CC Certificate
Don't Take Possession Without These!

Two critical documents that every flat buyer must demand before moving in. Here's what they mean and why they're non-negotiable.

📝 Buyer Education•July 30, 2026•5 min read

What is a Completion Certificate (CC)?

A Completion Certificate confirms that the building construction has been completed as per the sanctioned plan. It verifies that the developer followed the approved layout, maintained setbacks, adhered to FSI limits, and built within the permitted number of floors.

In simple terms: CC means the building is complete and built as per plan.

The CC is issued by the local municipal authority — in Hyderabad, this is typically GHMC, HMDA, or the respective Gram Panchayat depending on the project location.

What is an Occupancy Certificate (OC)?

An Occupancy Certificate goes one step further. It certifies that the building is safe for people to live in. The local authority inspects fire safety systems, water supply, sewage connections, structural stability, and electrical safety before issuing an OC.

In simple terms: OC means the building is safe to occupy.

The OC is issued after the CC — meaning a building must first be certified as complete, then certified as habitable.

CC vs OC – Key Differences

  • CC: Construction is complete as per sanctioned plan
  • OC: Building is safe and fit for human occupancy
  • CC issued: After construction completion
  • OC issued: After safety inspection post-construction
  • CC confirms: Plan compliance (setbacks, FSI, floors)
  • OC confirms: Safety compliance (fire, water, structural, electrical)

Why These Certificates Matter – Practical Impact

Without OC and CC, you face real problems:

  • Home loan final disbursement: Banks will not release the final tranche of your loan without OC. You'll be stuck paying EMI on a partial loan with no possession.
  • Legal occupancy: Living in a building without OC is technically illegal. The municipal authority can issue notices or even seal the property.
  • Utility connections: Permanent water, electricity, and sewage connections from the government require OC. Without it, you'll depend on temporary connections that can be cut anytime.
  • Resale value: No buyer or bank will touch a property without OC during resale. Your flat becomes extremely difficult to sell.
  • Property tax: Without proper certificates, property tax assessment can be problematic, leading to legal complications later.

What Happens If a Building Doesn't Have OC/CC?

Many buyers move into flats without checking these documents because the developer hands over keys and says "everything is done." Here's what can go wrong:

  • Municipal authority can declare the building illegal and issue demolition notices
  • Insurance companies may refuse to cover a property without OC
  • You cannot get a khata transfer in your name without OC in many municipalities
  • Future property disputes become harder to resolve without proper documentation

How to Verify OC and CC

Always ask the developer for a copy of OC and CC before taking possession. You can independently verify by:

  • Visiting the local municipal office (GHMC/HMDA) with the building permission number
  • Checking online portals where available (GHMC has an online building permission tracker)
  • Asking your home loan bank — they will verify OC as part of final disbursement

Bottom line: Never pay the final installment or take possession keys without confirmed OC and CC. No matter how much the developer pressures you, these documents protect your life's biggest investment.
