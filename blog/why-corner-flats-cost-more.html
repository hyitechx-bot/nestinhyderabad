Why Corner Flats Cost More
Is the Premium Worth Paying?

Corner flats are always the first to sell and command a noticeable premium. But is the extra cost justified? Let's break down the real benefits and when to skip the premium.

📝 Buyer Education•July 30, 2026•5 min read

Why Corner Flats Command a Premium

In any apartment building, corner units (also called end units) sit at the junction of two sides. This positioning gives them unique advantages that middle units simply cannot offer:

The Key Benefits of Corner Flats

  • Cross Ventilation: With windows on 3 sides instead of 2, air flows naturally through the flat. In Hyderabad's humid climate, this reduces AC dependency significantly — saving you ₹2,000-4,000 monthly on electricity.
  • Natural Light from 3 Directions: More windows mean more sunlight throughout the day. Your flat feels brighter, more spacious, and needs fewer artificial lights during daytime.
  • No Shared Walls (One Side): At least one side has no neighbor. Less noise transfer, more privacy, and the psychological comfort of not hearing your neighbor's TV or conversations.
  • More Windows: Typically 2-4 extra windows compared to middle units. Better views, more ventilation options, and the ability to have windows in rooms that would otherwise be dark.
  • Often Slightly Larger: Some builders design corner units with 50-100 sqft extra carpet area due to the structural layout.

What's the Typical Premium?

In Hyderabad's apartment market, corner flats typically cost:

  • Budget projects (₹4,000-6,000/sqft): ₹50-100/sqft premium
  • Mid-segment (₹6,000-9,000/sqft): ₹100-150/sqft premium
  • Premium projects (₹9,000+/sqft): ₹150-200/sqft premium

For a 1,500 sqft flat, that translates to ₹75,000 to ₹3,00,000 extra. The question is whether this premium pays for itself.

When the Premium Is Absolutely Worth It

  • You're buying for self-use and plan to live there 10+ years (daily comfort compounds)
  • The flat faces an open area (garden, road) on the extra side — maximum benefit
  • You work from home and need natural light and ventilation for productivity
  • Higher floors where corner position gives panoramic views
  • You value privacy and hate neighbor noise

When a Regular Unit Is Fine

  • The corner side faces another building wall (you get a window but no view or useful light)
  • The premium exceeds ₹3 Lakhs and you're budget-constrained
  • You're buying purely for investment/rental — tenants rarely pay extra for corner position
  • Lower floors where surrounding structures block ventilation advantages anyway
  • The project has excellent overall ventilation design (all units have cross ventilation)

Pro Tip for Hyderabad Buyers

When evaluating a corner flat, visit the unit (or a similar floor) at different times of day. Check what the extra side actually faces — is it an open garden, a busy road (noise!), or another tower 20 feet away? The value of a corner flat depends entirely on what that extra exposure gives you. An open, green view is worth the premium. Staring at a wall or hearing traffic all day is not.
