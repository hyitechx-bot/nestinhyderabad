Site Visit Checklist – 5 Things to Check
Before Buying Any Flat

A site visit isn't just about seeing the model flat. Here's what experienced buyers actually check – and what you should never miss.

📝 Buyer Education•July 30, 2026•5 min read

Why a Proper Site Visit Matters More Than Brochures

Glossy brochures and 3D renders can make any project look like a five-star resort. But the reality on the ground tells a completely different story. A well-planned site visit can save you from investing in a property that looks great on paper but fails in daily living.

Here are the 5 critical things you must inspect during every site visit – plus tips on when and how to visit for maximum clarity.

1. Construction Quality – Look Beyond the Paint

The model flat is designed to impress. But the actual unit you'll receive may differ significantly. Here's what to check:

  • Walls: Tap on walls to check for hollow sounds (indicates poor plastering). Look for hairline cracks, dampness spots, or uneven surfaces
  • Tiles: Check if floor tiles are level (place a marble – it shouldn't roll). Look for chipped edges and uneven grout lines
  • Paint: Check for bubbling, peeling, or patches that indicate moisture issues
  • Windows & Doors: Open and close every door and window. Check for gaps, alignment issues, and quality of fittings
  • Plumbing: Turn on all taps, flush toilets, check water pressure and drainage speed
  • Electrical: Test all switches, check socket placements, and look at the quality of wiring in the DB box

2. Surroundings – What's Outside Your Window

You're not just buying a flat – you're buying into a neighbourhood. Step outside the project boundary and observe:

  • Drainage: Are open drains nearby? Do you see waterlogging signs? Check with locals about monsoon flooding
  • Roads: Is the approach road wide enough for two-way traffic? Is it a municipal road or a developer-built one?
  • Neighbours: What's being constructed nearby? A future commercial building, garbage dump, or busy highway can ruin your living experience
  • Noise levels: Visit during peak hours to assess traffic noise, construction sounds, or industrial activity
  • Odour check: Sewage treatment plants, garbage yards, or industrial areas nearby can make life miserable

3. Amenities Progress – Promises vs Reality

Builders advertise 30+ amenities, but many are completed years after possession (or never). During your site visit:

  • Check if the clubhouse structure is actually built or still a foundation
  • Is the swimming pool functional or just a pit in the ground?
  • Are landscaped gardens planted or still barren land?
  • Is the gym equipped or just an empty room?
  • Ask existing residents (if occupied) which amenities are actually functional

Red flag: If 80% of units are occupied but amenities aren't ready, the builder is unlikely to complete them soon.

4. Parking & Common Areas – The Daily Experience

You'll use parking and common areas every single day. Inspect them carefully:

  • Is your allocated parking slot accessible without squeezing past pillars?
  • Can you comfortably open car doors in the given space?
  • Are visitor parking spots available?
  • Check lift speed and number of lifts per tower (ideal: 2 lifts per 4 flats per floor)
  • Staircase width and lighting – important for emergencies
  • Garbage disposal system – is there a chute or do you carry bags to the ground floor?

5. Connectivity During Rush Hours

This is where most buyers make mistakes. They visit on a Sunday afternoon when roads are empty. Instead:

  • Drive to the site during Monday 9 AM traffic – that's your daily commute reality
  • Check evening traffic (6-8 PM) for the return journey
  • Time the actual drive to your workplace, schools, and hospitals
  • Check public transport availability – nearest metro, bus stop, auto stand
  • Verify Google Maps travel time during peak hours (don't trust the builder's "15 minutes from IT hub" claim)

Best Times to Visit a Property Site

  • Morning (7-9 AM): Check natural light in the flat, traffic on approach roads, school bus availability
  • Afternoon (12-2 PM): Check heat exposure, west-facing sun, ventilation quality
  • Evening (5-7 PM): Check street lighting, security presence, return commute time
  • Rainy day: Best time to check waterlogging, leaks, and drainage

Questions to Ask During Your Visit

  1. When is the expected possession date (in writing)?
  2. How many units are sold vs total units?
  3. What's the monthly maintenance charge per sqft?
  4. Is there a backup water source (borewell, tanker arrangement)?
  5. What's the power backup capacity (full or partial)?
  6. Can I speak to an existing resident?
